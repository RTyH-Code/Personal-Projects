# FireRed QoL + Hard

> **Spoiler guide:** every encounter table, item, trainer team and gift Pokémon is listed in [guide/](guide/README.md), plus a full [Pokédex](guide/POKEDEX.md) for all 386 Pokémon, [peak trainer prep](guide/PEAK.md) and a [type chart and move list](guide/TYPES.md), the [Trainer Tower teams](guide/TOWER.md) and the [VS Seeker rematch teams](guide/REMATCHES.md).

A quality-of-life, modernization and difficulty hack built on the pret/pokefirered decompilation.

## Quality of life
- Running Shoes work from the start (hold B).
- New games default to Fast text and the "Set" battle style.
- TMs are reusable, like in later generations. They can't be sold or tossed.
- HM moves can be forgotten at any time.
- **HMs without teaching them:** with the HM in your bag and the badge that
  allows it, Cut, Surf, Strength, Rock Smash and Waterfall work straight from
  the overworld, even if no Pokémon knows the move. Fly and Flash show up in
  the party menu on a Pokémon that could learn them (or your first Pokémon if
  none can).
- Trade evolutions without trading:
  - Kadabra, Machoke, Graveler and Haunter evolve at Lv. 37.
  - Use King's Rock on Poliwhirl or Slowpoke, Metal Coat on Onix or Scyther,
    Dragon Scale on Seadra, Up-Grade on Porygon, and Deep Sea Tooth or
    Deep Sea Scale on Clamperl, the same way you use an evolution stone.
- Evolutions FireRed can't trigger (it has no clock and no Pokéblocks):
  - Use a Sun Stone on Eevee for Espeon, or a Moon Stone for Umbreon.
  - Use a Deep Sea Scale on Feebas for Milotic.
- Fossils:
  - At Mt. Moon you can take both the Helix and the Dome Fossil.
  - A Root Fossil and a Claw Fossil are in Sevault Canyon (Seven Island).
  - The Cinnabar Lab revives every fossil you bring: Omanyte, Kabuto,
    Aerodactyl, Lileep and Anorith. Lileep and Anorith can also turn up
    by smashing rocks in Sevault Canyon.
- Celadon Department Store 4F sells every evolution item: all stones,
  including the Moon and Sun Stones, plus the trade items above.
- **Berry stand:** a clerk at the Celadon Department Store 2F counter sells
  berries, since FireRed has no berry growing. The stock grows as you
  progress:

  | When | Berries | Price |

  | From the start | Cheri, Chesto, Pecha, Rawst, Aspear, Persim | ₽200 |
  | | Oran | ₽100 |
  | | Leppa | ₽400 |

  | | Lum | ₽1,500 |

  The stand also sells herbs:
  - **From the start:** Energy Powder and Heal Powder.
  - **After Erika:** Energy Root, Revival Herb, White Herb and Mental Herb
    (the two held herbs are ₽2,000 each).
- **More battle consumables:** the X-item clerk on Celadon Department Store 5F
  adds Ether and Elixir after Erika, and Max Ether, Max Elixir and Max Revive
  after you become Champion. In the original game you could only find these.
- Pokémon from Johto and Hoenn can evolve before you have the National Dex.
- Stone evolutions keep learning moves by level-up. Modern games only give
  them level-1 moves, so the 18 affected Pokémon now learn their
  pre-evolution's later moves a few levels later instead, e.g. Ninetales
  learns Flamethrower at 37 (Vulpix: 32) and Fire Blast at 60 (Vulpix: 52).
- **Battle speed:** Options > BATTLE SPEED. FAST, the default, drains HP and
  fills Exp. bars faster and halves the pause after each battle message.
  Choose NORMAL for vanilla speed.
- **Release number:** the Options screen shows which release you're playing
  (for example, "QoL+HARD R12"). It's handy when reporting a bug.
- **Repel prompt:** when a Repel wears off and you have another of the same
  kind, the game asks whether to use it.
- **Modern shiny odds:** shiny Pokémon appear at 1 in 4,096 (up from
  1 in 8,192). After you become Champion, Prof. Oak's aide (by the lab door)
  gives you the **Shiny Charm**. While it's in your bag, each new wild, gift or
  one-time Pokémon gets two extra chances to be shiny (about 1 in 1,365).

## Move relearner
The Move Maniac on Two Island works as usual (for mushrooms) until you
become Champion. After that he's free and can teach any move the Pokémon can
learn: level-up moves at any level, egg moves, every compatible TM/HM, and
move tutor moves (including Frenzy Plant, Blast Burn and Hydro Cannon), and
every move the modern tutor can teach it.

## Deoxys formes
- Deoxys has all four formes: Normal, Attack, Defense and Speed. Each has its
  own sprite, icon and stats.
- The Deoxys you catch on Birth Island starts in its Normal Forme.
- Catching it gives you the **Meteorite**. Use it on Deoxys from the Key
  Items pocket to cycle Normal → Attack → Defense → Speed.
- The Speed Forme sprites come from pret's Pokémon Emerald decompilation.

## IV/EV editor
On a Pokémon's summary, go to the **POKéMON SKILLS** page and press **START**.

| Button | Action |
|---|---|
| Up / Down | Choose a stat |
| A | Switch between the IV and EV columns |
| Left / Right (IV column) | Set the IV (0–31) |
| Left / Right (EV column) | Move 4 EVs out of the stat into the pool / from the pool into the stat |
| START | Save. Only allowed when the EV pool is 0 |
| B | Cancel without saving |

The memo line shows the **Hidden Power** type and power your IVs give, and
updates as you change them. You can set IVs freely. EVs can only be moved between stats, so the total
you earned never goes up. Each stat is capped at 255. The editor works for
party and PC Pokémon, but not during battles, trades or link play.

## Nature and ability coach
Once you're Champion, an old woman in the Indigo Plateau Pokémon Center (next
to the League official) changes a party Pokémon's nature or ability. It's free
and you can use her as often as you like.
- **Nature:** pick the stat to raise and the stat to lower (or Neutral).
  Stats update at once. Gender, shininess, Unown's letter and Wurmple's
  evolution don't change.
- **Ability:** switches a Pokémon to its species' other ability, if it has two.

## Modern battle mechanics
- **Physical/special split.** Each move has its own category (from Gen 4
  onward), so Shadow Ball is special and Fire Punch is physical. Thick Fat now
  also halves Attack against Fire/Ice moves.
- **Modern base stats.** For example, Pidgeot has 101 Speed, Dugtrio 100
  Attack and Golem 120 Attack.
- **Modern move power, accuracy and PP.** For example, Flamethrower and
  Thunderbolt are 90 power, Leech Life is 80 and Knock Off is 65. Fury Cutter
  is capped at 160 power, as in modern games. Move *effects* are still Gen 3.
- **Modern Exp. formula (Gen 5+).** Exp. depends on the level gap: beating a
  foe above your Pokémon's level gives more Exp. and beating one below gives
  less, so a team that falls behind catches up faster and over-levelling is
  slower. Compared with Gen 3:
  - A foe at the same level gives 1.4x the Exp.
  - A foe 10 levels higher gives about 1.7x to 2x.
  - A much weaker foe gives less. A Lv. 14 Pokémon gets 23 Exp. for a wild
    Lv. 5 Rattata (it was 40).
- **Modern level-up learnsets**, taken from the newest generation that has
  each Pokémon (Gen 9, then 8, then 7), limited to moves that exist in Gen 3.
- **Fairy type (Gen 6).** Fairy hits Fighting, Dragon and Dark super
  effectively and is resisted by Fire, Poison and Steel; Dragon moves don't
  affect it, and Poison and Steel moves hit it hard. As in Gen 6, Steel no
  longer resists Ghost or Dark.
  - Clefairy, Jigglypuff, Togepi, Snubbull, Marill and Ralts (and their
    families), Mr. Mime and Mawile have their modern Fairy typing.
  - Charm, Sweet Kiss and Moonlight are Fairy-type.
  - Six new Fairy attacks: Fairy Wind, Disarming Voice, Draining Kiss (heals
    3/4 of the damage), Dazzling Gleam (hits both foes), Play Rough and
    Moonblast. Pokémon learn them by level-up or as egg moves where modern
    games teach them that way.
  - Lance's Dragons carry Iron Tail so they aren't helpless against Fairies.

## Later moves
- 143 moves from Gen 4 onward. 74 of them reuse Gen 3 effects, such as
  Night Slash, Dark Pulse, Drain Punch, Aura Sphere, Focus Blast, Bullet
  Punch, Poison Jab, Gunk Shot, Earth Power, Stone Edge, X-Scissor, Bug Buzz,
  Shadow Claw, Shadow Sneak, Flash Cannon, Iron Head, Zen Headbutt, Psychic
  Fangs, Aqua Jet, Scald, Liquidation, Energy Ball, Leaf Storm, Wood Hammer,
  Discharge, Wild Charge, Ice Shard, Icicle Crash, Dragon Pulse, Draco Meteor,
  Air Slash, Brave Bird, Boomburst, Nasty Plot and Rock Polish.
  - Their power, accuracy, PP, priority and category are modern.
  - Animations are reused from existing moves, tinted in the move's type
    colour.
- 37 more moves with new effects:
  - Set-up: Quiver Dance, Shell Smash, Coil, Hone Claws, Work Up, Cotton Guard.
  - Self stat changes after the hit: Close Combat, Hammer Arm, Flame Charge,
    Charge Beam, Trailblaze.
  - Recoil and extra effects: Head Smash (1/2 recoil), Flare Blitz (recoil
    and 10% burn), Fire/Ice/Thunder Fang (10% status and 10% flinch), Acid
    Spray (sharply lowers Sp. Def).
  - Power that depends on the battle: Hex, Venoshock, Brine, Acrobatics,
    Stored Power, Power Trip, Gyro Ball, Electro Ball, Heavy Slam, Heat
    Crash, Payback.
  - Other stats: Psyshock and Psystrike hit Defense, Foul Play uses the
    target's Attack, Body Press uses the user's Defense.
  - Freeze-Dry is super effective on Water types.
  - Frost Breath and Storm Throw always land a critical hit.
  - Hurricane never misses in rain (50% in sun) and may confuse.
  - Sucker Punch strikes first but fails unless the target is about to
    attack.
- 32 more moves that needed new battle systems or effects:
  - U-turn, Volt Switch and Flip Turn switch the user out after the hit.
  - Tailwind doubles your side's Speed for 4 turns.
  - Trick Room makes slower Pokémon move first for 5 turns (using it again
    ends it).
  - Entry hazards hit Pokémon as they switch in:
    - Stealth Rock does 1/8 HP, scaled by Rock-type effectiveness.
    - Toxic Spikes poisons (two layers: badly poisons); a grounded Poison
      type absorbs them.
    - Sticky Web lowers Speed.
  - Defog clears hazards on both sides and the target's Reflect, Light
    Screen, Safeguard and Mist, and lowers evasion. Rapid Spin clears the
    new hazards too.
  - Dragon Tail and Circle Throw drag out a random foe in trainer battles
    and end wild battles, like Roar.
  - Feint breaks through Protect and Detect.
  - Poltergeist fails if the target holds no item.
  - Fell Stinger drastically raises Attack if it knocks out the target.
  - Dragon Ascent lowers the user's defenses.
  - Wring Out and Hard Press are stronger the more HP the target has.
    Punishment is stronger the more the target has raised its stats.
  - Others: Sludge Wave, Inferno, Incinerate, Triple Axel, Origin Pulse
    (Kyogre), Precipice Blades (Groudon), High Horsepower, Brutal Swing,
    Skitter Smack, Pounce, Chilling Water, Ice Spinner, Supercell Slam.
  - Roost heals half HP. It doesn't remove the Flying type for the turn.
- Knock Off is modern: 65 power, 1.5x if the target holds an item.
- Pokémon learn them by level-up and as egg moves wherever modern games
  teach them that way.
- **Modern tutor:** the old man in the Viridian City Pokémon Center teaches,
  for free, any of these moves (and the Fairy moves) that the Pokémon could
  learn by TM or tutor in Gen 4–9.
  - Moves with 70+ power, and the strongest set-up moves (Nasty Plot, Quiver
    Dance, Shell Smash, Coil, Cotton Guard), need 3 badges.
  - Moves with 90+ power need 5 badges.
- The Pokédex guide lists every Pokémon's modern tutor moves.

## Modern abilities
- Every Pokémon can have up to three abilities, as in modern games: two
  regular ones and a hidden ability. Wild Pokémon get one of the regular ones;
  the coach in the Indigo Plateau Pokémon Center (after you become Champion)
  can switch a Pokémon to any of them, including the hidden one.
- All 74 abilities that later games gave Gen 1–3 Pokémon work, from
  Adaptability to Wonder Skin, including Mold Breaker, Magic Bounce, Imposter,
  Protean (once per switch-in, as in Gen 9), Neutralizing Gas, Prankster
  (Dark types are immune to its boosted moves) and Infiltrator.
- Honey Gather is the one exception (FireRed has no Honey), so Teddiursa keeps
  its other abilities.
- Pickpocket steals for the rest of the battle only; items go back afterwards.
- Two newer abilities for the later evolutions: Motor Drive (Electivire:
  Electric moves raise its Speed instead of hurting it) and Armor Tail
  (Farigiraf: foes' priority moves fail against its side).
- Critical hits still do double damage (Gen 3); Sniper makes them triple.
- Sun, rain and Flash Fire now boost physical Fire and Water moves too (with
  the physical/special split, moves like Fire Punch had been missing out).
- The original Gen 3 abilities now work the modern way:
  - Sturdy: also survives any hit at 1 HP when at full HP.
  - Lightning Rod: makes the holder immune to Electric moves and raises its Sp. Atk.
  - Drizzle, Drought and Sand Stream: weather lasts 5 turns instead of forever.
  - Intimidate: Inner Focus, Own Tempo, Oblivious and Scrappy block it.
  - Oblivious: also blocks Taunt.
  - Keen Eye: ignores the target's evasion boosts.
  - Rough Skin: does 1/8 damage.
  - Effect Spore: Grass types and Overcoat are immune.
  - Plus and Minus: power up with a partner that has either ability.
  - Stench: 10% chance to make the target flinch.
  - Trace: can't copy Trace, Imposter, Neutralizing Gas or Forecast.
  - Trapping: Ghost types can always run or switch out (except when rooted by
    Ingrain). Shadow Tag doesn't trap another Shadow Tag.

## Difficulty
### Challenge settings and Nuzlocke mode
When you wake up in your room at the start of a new game, a menu lets you
switch on any of these rules. Pick a rule to turn it ON or OFF, then choose
START ADVENTURE:

| Rule | What it does |
|---|---|
| Nuzlocke mode | The core Nuzlocke rules below. |
| Level caps | The level caps described in the next section. |
| No items in battle | You can't use items from the Bag in battle. Poké Balls still work. |
| Set battle style | No free switch after you knock out a foe. The battle style option is locked to Set. |
| Party Exp. Share | Not a challenge: your whole team gets Exp. (see below). |

The rules are chosen once, for the whole run. Level caps and Party Exp. Share
are the exceptions: the scientist in the Viridian City Pokémon Center can
switch either on or off at any time, including on saves from before these
options existed.

**Party Exp. Share** works like the modern Exp. Share:
- Every Pokémon that battled gets the full Exp.
- The rest of the team gets half.
- The Exp. formula still scales with each Pokémon's own level.
- Level caps still apply.

It makes the game much easier, so it's off by default. For example, beating a
wild Lv. 9 Rattata with a Lv. 50 lead gives the lead 11 Exp. and a benched
Lv. 5 Pokémon 75.

**Nuzlocke mode** starts once Prof. Oak gives you your Poké Balls:
- **First encounter only:** you can only catch the first wild Pokémon you meet
  in each area. That includes grass, caves, water, fishing and the Safari Zone.
  Once an area is used, Poké Balls won't work there. The game tells you and
  the ball isn't used up (a Safari Ball still is).
- **Dupes clause:** if that first Pokémon is in an evolution family you
  already own, it doesn't count, so the area stays open until you find a
  new one.
- **Shiny clause:** a shiny Pokémon can always be caught.
- **One-time Pokémon** like Snorlax and the legendaries can always be caught
  and don't use up the area.
- **Fainting is permanent:** a Pokémon that faints can't be healed or revived
  by Pokémon Centers, items or anything else, even after a trip to the PC.
- **Tracker:** while Nuzlocke mode is on, the Start menu has a NUZLOCKE entry.
  It lists every area you've used, with what you caught there and whether it's
  in your party, in the PC, fainted or missed (ran away, fainted or failed to
  catch).
- **Blacking out ends the run:** the Nuzlocke rules are lifted and the game
  carries on as a normal playthrough.
- **Exhibition battles don't count:** in the Trainer Tower and against the
  Lv. 100 peak trainers (Gym Leader rematches and Elite Four round 3),
  Pokémon that faint aren't lost (they're left with 1 HP), and losing
  doesn't end the run. If you black out in one, the game tells you the run
  goes on.

### Optional level caps
Talk to the scientist in the Viridian City Pokémon Center to turn level caps on
or off at any time. While they're on, your Pokémon can't gain Exp. (or Rare
Candy levels) above the highest level on the next Gym Leader's team you
haven't beaten. Like a Lv. 100 Pokémon, a capped Pokémon also gains no EVs from
battle. The caps are lifted once you become Champion.

| Next opponent | Cap |
|---|---|
| Brock | 16 |
| Misty | 23 |
| Lt. Surge | 28 |
| Erika | 35 |
| Koga | 45 |
| Sabrina | 47 |
| Blaine | 51 |
| Giovanni | 54 |
| Elite Four and Champion | 72 |

If you take gyms out of order, the cap is the lowest one among the gyms you
haven't beaten.

How the cap works with each way of gaining levels:
- **Battle Exp.:** a Pokémon below the cap gains only enough Exp. to reach it,
  even from one big award (Exp. Share and Lucky Egg included). It doesn't
  overshoot. A Pokémon already at or above the cap gains nothing.
- **Rare Candy and Special Candy:** these won't work on a Pokémon at the cap.
  The game tells you the current cap, and the candy isn't used up.
- **Day Care:** both Day Cares stop raising a Pokémon at the cap. The fee is
  based on the levels it actually gained.
- **Evolution:** this still happens on any level-up up to the cap. If you
  cancel an evolution at the cap, it's offered again on the Pokémon's next
  level-up once the cap rises.

### Trainers
- Every Gym Leader has a new, larger team with max IVs, better moves and a held item.
- Every other trainer's levels are scaled by 1.15x.
- Wild Pokémon from the Erika stretch on are also 1.15x higher, so training
  on them keeps pace with the trainers. This covers Routes 7–21, Rock Tunnel,
  Pokémon Tower, the Safari Zone, Power Plant, Seafoam Islands, Pokémon
  Mansion, the Sevii Islands 1–3, Route 23, Victory Road (now up to Lv. 55)
  and Cerulean Cave. Fishing levels are unchanged.
- **VS Seeker rematches catch up:**
  - **When:** until you have all eight badges.
  - **What:** any route rematch Pokémon below 5 levels under the next Gym
    Leader's strongest is raised to that level.
  - **Evolution:** it also evolves if its new level allows, so you won't
    meet a Lv. 40 Rattata.
  - **Why:** this keeps rematches worth fighting when you train before
    Koga, Sabrina, Blaine and Giovanni.
- **Lucky Egg from Koga:** after his gym, Koga also gives you a Lucky Egg.
  It's handy for bringing a Pokémon that has fallen behind up to speed.
  Saves that already beat him can talk to him again to get it.
- **Gym double battles:** one trainer in each gym from Misty's on (except
  Cinnabar) fights two-on-two, with a team built for doubles:
  - Spread moves that hit both of your Pokémon, such as Twister, Electroweb,
    Razor Leaf, Rock Slide and Dazzling Gleam.
  - Support such as Fake Out, Protect, screens, Trick Room and Helping Hand.
  - They never use moves that would hit their own partner.

  The trainers are Swimmer Luis, Engineer Baily, Beauty Bridget, Tamer Edgar,
  Psychic Johan and Cool Trainer Samuel. You need two Pokémon that can battle
  to take them on.
- **Victory Road rematches:** the VS Seeker now works in Victory Road, and
  all 12 of its trainers have rematch teams 8 levels higher (Lv. 54–63, evolved
  where the level allows). Like other VS Seeker rematches, you can fight them
  again each time the battery recharges (100 steps).
- Regular trainers have at least ~18 IVs. The Elite Four, Champion, Rival and Giovanni have max IVs.
- Every trainer uses the smart battle AI.
- From Lt. Surge on, the Gym Leaders, Elite Four and Champion use some of
  the newer moves, so their teams keep up with what you can now learn.
  - Examples: Nuzzle, Volt Switch, Venoshock, Dazzling Gleam, Hex, Flare
    Blitz, Stone Edge, Shell Smash, Close Combat, Stealth Rock, Hurricane.
  - Brock and Misty are unchanged, and no levels changed.
- The AI won't waste turns on later moves that would do nothing, such as
  hazards that are already up, Tailwind or Trick Room while they're active,
  or set-up moves at +2.

### Balance of the newer content
- **Leech Life** is 80 power now (it was 20), so Pokémon learn it as in modern
  games: Absorb early, Leech Life later (e.g. Zubat at 55, Venonat at 35). As
  in Gen 9, it's no longer an egg move. A Team Rocket Grunt's Lv. 20 Zubat
  use Absorb instead. Other moves that got stronger (Fury Cutter, Rapid Spin,
  Knock Off, Thrash, Petal Dance...) are learned at their modern levels.
- **Modern tutor tiers:**
  - Moves whose power depends on the battle are tiered by how hard they
    really hit. Hex, Venoshock, Brine, Acrobatics, Gyro Ball, Electro Ball,
    Heavy Slam, Heat Crash, Grass Knot, Payback, Avalanche, Wring Out, Hard
    Press and Triple Axel need 5 badges.
  - Strong utility moves need 3 badges: Nuzzle, Stealth Rock, Toxic Spikes,
    Sticky Web, Trick Room, Tailwind, Roost, Sucker Punch, Foul Play, Body
    Press, Psyshock, Flame Charge, Trailblaze, Hone Claws, Work Up, Stored
    Power, Power Trip, Punishment and Fell Stinger.
- **Evolution items:**
  - The stones (including Dusk, Shiny, Dawn and Ice) are sold at the
    Celadon Dept. Store 4F from the start.
  - The newer held evolution items (Razor Claw, Razor Fang, Protector,
    Electirizer, Magmarizer, Dubious Disc, Reaper Cloth, Peat Block and
    Black Augurite) appear there once you have 5 badges.

## Post-game peak trainers
- **Gym Leader rematches:** once you're Champion, talk to any Gym Leader
  for a rematch, as many times as you like. Each one fields six Lv. 100
  Pokémon that fit their type, with modern moves and some of the later
  evolutions, e.g. Brock: Golem, Omastar, Kabutops, Probopass, Rhyperior
  and Tyranitar.
- **Elite Four round 3:** a League official (gentleman) in the Indigo Plateau
  Pokémon Center switches the "peak" challenge on or off. When it's on,
  the Elite Four and Champion each use six Lv. 100 Pokémon.
- **Peak Pokémon:** they have perfect IVs, a nature that boosts the side
  their moves use (physical or special), 252 EVs in that attack stat, and
  252 in Speed (or in HP for slow Pokémon). Each peak trainer carries Full
  Restores.
- **Gym rematch rewards:** each time you beat a Gym Leader's rematch, they
  give you a Nugget (35%), PP Up (25%), PP Max (15%) or Star Piece (25%).
- **Elite Four round 3 rewards:** each peak Elite Four member gives the
  same reward as a Gym Leader rematch. Each time you beat the peak Champion, the
  League gives you a Master Ball (20%), 3 PP Max (30%), 3 Star Pieces (25%)
  or 5 Nuggets (25%).

## Trainer Tower (Seven Island)
Every Trainer Tower trainer now uses competitive sets, built the way real
teams are:
- **Fully evolved:** every Pokémon, using the later evolutions where they
  exist (e.g. Magmortar, Togekiss, Scizor, Porygon-Z). There's one of each
  species per team. When a trainer's family runs out (say, three Goldeen),
  another fully evolved Pokémon of the same type stands in.
- **Moves from real roles:** they come from Pokémon Showdown's role-based
  sets (Fast Attacker, Setup Sweeper, Bulky Support and so on), limited to
  moves in this hack that the Pokémon can learn.
  - On Double floors, the sets come from the doubles format, with Protect,
    spread moves, Helping Hand, Follow Me and Trick Room.
- **EVs, nature and IVs:** 252/252 EVs and a nature that suit the role, and
  perfect IVs.
- **Ability and item:** a fitting ability, and a held item for the role
  (Choice Band, Leftovers, Lum or Sitrus Berry, or a type booster), with no
  item repeated on a team.

- **Level 50 rule:** your Pokémon above Lv. 50 battle as Lv. 50, with
  their stats recalculated, and the opponents are all Lv. 50. Your team goes
  back to its real level after each battle, keeping the same share of its HP.
- **A prize every clear:** you get a prize each time you reach the Owner, not
  just the first time. It's the floor set's usual prize (25%), PP Up (20%),
  PP Max (10%), Nugget (20%), Big Pearl (15%) or Star Piece (10%).
- **Record bonus:** beat your best time for a challenge (your first clear
  counts) and the Owner adds a PP Max (40%), 2 Star Pieces (30%), 3 Rare
  Candies (20%) or a Master Ball (10%).
- **Treasure trader:** the man in the lobby swaps treasures for items you
  can't buy: 3 Star Pieces for a Master Ball, 2 Big Pearls or 3 PP Ups for a
  PP Max, or a Nugget for 2 Rare Candies. He trades one Master Ball per
  Trainer Tower clear.

## Post-game difficulty and held items
- **Sevii Islands 4–7:** these are the islands you reach after becoming
  Champion, and they now bridge the gap between the first Champion
  (Lv. 66–72) and the round 2 Elite Four (Lv. 72–86):
  - Their trainers and rematch teams are 1.3x stronger (about Lv. 57–82).
  - Their wild Pokémon are 1.35x stronger.
  - Trainer Pokémon evolve where their new level allows.
- **Post-game route rematches:** VS Seeker rematch teams that unlock after
  the Champion are 1.25x stronger (about Lv. 66–86).
- **Late-game evolutions:** every trainer Pokémon of Lv. 50 or higher has
  evolved where its level allows (5 levels past the evolution), even if the
  team already has one, e.g. no more Lv. 72 Mareep or Lv. 68 Magikarp.
  They also have their classic stone, item, trade and friendship
  evolutions (Kingdra, Crobat, Steelix, Blissey, Raichu and so on). The
  later-generation ones (Rhyperior, Magnezone, ...) are left alone below
  Lv. 75, since those items are badge-gated. The toughest teams (Lv. 75+)
  have them too, as a last step before the round 2 Elite Four. Earlier
  trainers keep their classic unevolved Pokémon.
- **Held items:** the Trainer Tower lobby shop on Seven Island also sells the
  held items competitive teams use: Leftovers, Choice Band, Shell Bell, Focus
  Band, Quick Claw, Scope Lens, Bright Powder, King's Rock, Lucky Egg, Metal
  Coat, Silk Scarf and every type-boosting item (₽3,000–10,000).

## True endgame (after beating the round 2 Champion)
The Champion's rematch (round 2) unlocks once the Sevii Islands story is
done. The first time you beat it, or the peak round 3, you get:
- **Special Candy:** a key item that works like a Rare Candy but is never
  used up. Use it as often as you like.
- **Free vitamins:** HP Up, Protein, Iron, Calcium, Zinc and Carbos cost
  ₽0 at the Celadon Dept. Store. One use now fills that stat's EVs up to
  the base game's caps: 255 per stat and 510 in total.
  Use the IV/EV editor to spread them exactly how you want.

## Building from source

The source changes are git patches on top of
[pret/pokefirered](https://github.com/pret/pokefirered) commit `ce6406d`.

```bash
git clone https://github.com/pret/pokefirered && cd pokefirered
git checkout ce6406d
git am /path/to/firered-qol-hard/source-patches/*.patch
make        # needs agbcc + arm-none-eabi binutils; see pret's INSTALL.md
```

The generator scripts that produced the data changes are in `tools/`. They're
also included in the patches under `tools/hack/`. Each one runs once against
unmodified pret data:
- `difficulty.py`: gym teams, trainer levels and IVs, AI.
- `modernize.py <pokemon-showdown>/data`: split categories, stats, moves and
  learnsets. It reads Pokémon Showdown's data files as text and never runs them.
- `encounters.py`: wild placements. It checks that all 386 Pokémon are obtainable.
- `legendaries.py`: legendary events, flags and the Hall of Fame tickets.
- `fairy.py <pokemon-showdown>/data`: the Fairy type, retyped Pokémon, Fairy moves and their learnsets.
- `abilities.py <pokemon-showdown>/data`: ability slots, new ability names and each Pokémon's modern abilities (`--species` re-assigns them).
- `evo_moves.py`: later level-up moves for stone evolutions (run after `modernize.py`).
- `add_species.py <pokemon-showdown>/data <expansion> NAME`: adds a later Pokémon in an unused species slot, with its graphics, cry, Pokédex entry and evolution item.
- `moves.py <pokemon-showdown>/data <batch>`: the later moves, the modern tutor and its data; `moves_engine_b2.py` and `moves_engine_b3.py` add the battle effects batches 2 and 3 need.
- `balance.py`: modern boss movesets and peak teams with the later evolutions.
- `validate.py`: checks the game data for missing or inconsistent entries.
- `peak_trainers.py`: peak teams, gym rematches and the Elite Four round 3.
- `spoiler_guide.py <dir>`: regenerates the spoiler guide from the game data.
- `tower_sets.py <dir>`: the competitive Trainer Tower teams, from Showdown's random-battle set files (read as data).
- `playtest/`: an automated playtest (a headless mGBA harness plus 25 milestone checks, from the first battles to the post-game rewards).
- `gym_doubles.py`: the gym double battles and their doubles movesets.
- `learnsets.py`: what each species can learn in this hack (used by the trainer scripts).
- `vr_rematches.py`: the Victory Road VS Seeker rematch teams and scripts.
- `consumables_shop.py`: herbs, PP restorers and Max Revive in the Celadon shops (run after `berry_shop.py`).
- `berry_shop.py`: the Celadon Department Store berry stand and berry prices.
- `held_item_shop.py`: held items in the post-game Trainer Tower lobby shop.
- `postgame_levels.py`: Sevii Islands 4–7 trainers and wild Pokémon, and post-game route rematches.
- `trainer_evolutions.py`: Lv. 50+ trainer Pokémon evolve where their level allows, then by classic stone, item, trade or friendship evolutions, then any evolution at Lv. 75+ (run after `postgame_levels.py`).
- `wild_levels.py`: wild Pokémon levels 1.15x from the Erika stretch on.
- `exp_budget.py`: how much trainer Exp. each stretch of the game gives, against the level caps.
- `save_check.py REF`: checks whether saves from an older release (a git ref) still load.
